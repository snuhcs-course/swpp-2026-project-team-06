### Discussion

**Producer findings**

- Little experience with pre-orders
- Producers rarely take photos
- → Follow-up phone interviews on these two points after the holiday (Tuesday)

**Consumer findings**

- Priorities: taste/sweetness, freshness/quality, and price come first
- Little experience buying directly from farms
- The main barrier to pre-ordering is not knowing the quality in advance
- Shorter waiting periods are better → needs further validation
- Room to split roles between producer communication and AI guidance
- Among features, growing/harvest content was the most attractive
- Little interest in specific varieties; quality matters more than the growing story

**Consumer target**

- Priority order: **taste/sweetness >> freshness = quality > price**
- Target fruit: fruit where taste/sweetness is the main value
- Age group
  - 20s–30s: little direct-trade experience, may not know where to buy, dislike searching for information themselves
  - 40s–50s: purchasing power and decision-making authority, likely to engage more with farmers (heavy users); 20s–30s act as indirect users (e.g., ordering for parents)
- Gender: leaning toward women

**Premium vs. value**

- Mapped price level (low / normal / high) against quality level; the survey points to **high quality at a normal price** as the main need
- Two possible user funnels
  1. **High price, high quality** — rare, high-preference varieties sold in small volumes (not mandarins). Smaller user base and sales make it easier to control early on, and it supports producers of diverse varieties. Can expand to funnel 2 later
  2. **Normal price, high quality** — widely eaten fruit (mandarins). Affects more users, but mandarins are hard to position as premium
- Selling above common online retail prices was rejected: consumers will not buy

**Mandarin direction**

- Handle all varieties (variety-agnostic) and focus on high-quality produce
- Quality definition
  - Sugar content (Brix) as the key criterion — farms can measure it destructively at harvest; few farms have non-destructive meters
  - Otherwise follow general grading standards (appearance, size)
  - Mixing off-standard produce could create price competitiveness, but options cannot be added mid-growth; decide during production or run it as an event → needs an additional survey

**Online marketing**

- Goal before the next iteration: test interest, validate decided hypotheses, and resolve open ones
- Typical channels (search ads, shopping ads, display ads) are more expensive than expected, and food ads are mostly video → proceed in stages
  1. Find evidence in the user study that it is worth proceeding
  2. Define exactly which hypotheses online marketing must test
  3. Then run marketing
- Alternative: run our own Instagram/YouTube accounts that reach the 40s–50s target

### Decisions

- Revise surveys based on the findings and run follow-up producer interviews
- Consumer target: fruit valued for taste/sweetness, high quality at a normal price; do not price above common retail
- Product direction: mandarins, all varieties, quality defined primarily by sugar content
- Online marketing proceeds in stages; owned social channels considered as an alternative to paid ads
- Open: whether evidence is sufficient for targets and spec/BM, whether MOQ is achievable, and whether hypotheses are validated
